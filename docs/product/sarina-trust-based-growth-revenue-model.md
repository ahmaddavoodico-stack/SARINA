# SARINA Trust-Based Growth & Revenue Model

**Status:** Product strategy approved; implementation policy  
**Applies to:** Farmer experience, AI assistant, Green Credit, marketplace, experts, commercial partners, marketing, and future regional operations  
**Core promise:** “No farmer is alone.”  
**Commercial principle:** “We earn from farmers’ trust, not from their need or hardship.”

## 1. Purpose

SARINA is designed to create durable economic value for farmers. Its growth model must make useful foundational services accessible before a farmer spends money. A farmer who does not purchase is not a failed customer or a user to discard: they may still benefit from SARINA, participate in the agricultural community, and recommend the service to others.

Revenue should arise primarily from optional products and services that customers knowingly choose because they provide clear value—not from withholding essential help, creating artificial dependency, or exploiting urgent needs.

This is a product and operating principle, not merely a marketing statement.

## 2. The trust-to-growth loop

SARINA should build and measure the following cycle:

1. Deliver useful, reliable, accessible foundational value.
2. Earn satisfaction through honest, respectful service.
3. Build trust through consistency, transparency, and farmer control.
4. Enable voluntary word-of-mouth referrals and community participation.
5. Grow the farmer network and create more useful marketplace connections.
6. Earn sustainable revenue from optional products, expert services, and successful commercial transactions.
7. Reinvest in service quality, access, resilience, and farmer outcomes.

Referral activity must be voluntary. No spam, coercive invitations, misleading claims, or rewards that encourage fake accounts.

## 3. Three service and revenue layers

### Layer A — Universal foundational services

These services should remain available without requiring a purchase:

- Core conversational agricultural assistance, subject to fair and transparent infrastructure safeguards.
- Farmer and farm-profile creation and management.
- Access to useful agricultural information and general guidance.
- Basic marketplace discovery and access to relevant market information.
- Clear pathways to human experts and other support, without making purchase a condition for receiving basic AI help.

Free access does not mean unlimited or costless infrastructure. SARINA may apply reasonable rate limits, abuse prevention, context/token management, and graceful service degradation. Such controls must be proportionate, explained respectfully, and must not discriminate against farmers who do not buy.

### Layer B — Optional expert and professional services

Revenue may be earned from clearly described services selected by the farmer, such as:

- Paid consultations with verified agricultural experts.
- Farm visits and specialist field services.
- Laboratory soil, water, and plant analysis.
- Advanced reports or other professional services with a clear scope and price.

Prices, provider identity, deliverables, cancellation terms, and any platform fee must be disclosed before commitment. Human expert fees must remain distinct from AI usage credits.

### Layer C — Marketplace and commercial ecosystem

Potential revenue sources include:

- Sales of SARINA-owned products.
- Clearly identified third-party products from qualified suppliers.
- Transparent commissions on completed transactions where applicable.
- Business partnerships and services for companies, buyers, processors, and institutions.
- Voluntary commercial services that create measurable value for participants.

Farmers should retain meaningful choice, transparent pricing, and direct access to buyers where feasible. Commercial partners must not receive privileged access to private farmer data without an appropriate lawful basis and user permission.

## 4. Non-negotiable trust safeguards

### 4.1 Independent agricultural advice

The AI assistant must recommend according to the farmer's actual needs, farm context, evidence, safety, and suitability—not according to commission, inventory, supplier payment, or SARINA's margin.

If a suitable product is available in the SARINA marketplace, it may be shown with clear commercial disclosure. If a better option is unavailable or sold elsewhere, the assistant must say so honestly. Paid placement must never masquerade as agronomic advice.

### 4.2 No purchase-gated basic help

A farmer must not be denied foundational assistance, treated as less deserving, or pressured to buy because they have not purchased before. Promotions and reward offers must be optional and must not interrupt essential help.

### 4.3 Honest claims and transparent terms

SARINA must not promise guaranteed yield increases, guaranteed income, or unsupported product outcomes. Recommendations should distinguish farmer-reported information, measured data, estimates, and assumptions. Commercial relationships and sponsored placements must be clearly labeled.

### 4.4 Privacy and farmer control

Collect only data needed for the service. Explain how farm and transaction data are used. Do not sell identifiable farmer data. Sharing with experts, buyers, suppliers, or partners must follow explicit permissions and applicable law. Farmers should be able to understand and control their sharing choices.

### 4.5 Fairness and accessibility

Design for different farm sizes, income levels, literacy levels, languages, connectivity conditions, and device capabilities. Avoid making paid features the only practical route to essential information. Preserve offline-tolerant workflows where technically feasible.

## 5. Green Credit alignment

SARINA Green Credit supports sustainable access to AI infrastructure; it must not become a disguised paywall.

- Every farmer receives a clearly defined free base allowance, with its amount and renewal policy disclosed.
- Usage is measured from actual model processing (including relevant input/output tokens and billable tools), not simply message count.
- Base free credit is separate from promotional or earned bonus credit.
- Purchases may provide explicitly disclosed bonus credit, but buying is never required to receive the base allowance.
- Educational and community contributions may qualify for transparent rewards under published rules.
- Human expert compensation is separate from AI credits.
- Expiry, rollover, refunds, reversals, abuse prevention, and renewal rules must be defined before production launch.
- When usage is high or credit is exhausted, communicate respectfully and offer usage-management options; do not shame or pressure the farmer to purchase.

## 6. Voluntary referral and community growth

Build referral features only when they add genuine value:

- Optional personal invitation links or codes.
- Clear explanation of any reward and its eligibility conditions.
- Rewards for verified, meaningful participation rather than raw invitation volume alone.
- Anti-fraud protections that do not unfairly penalize legitimate farmers.
- No unsolicited bulk messaging or forced contact uploads.
- No requirement to invite others to access foundational services.

Referral rewards must be budgeted as a customer-acquisition cost and evaluated against retained, satisfied users—not treated as free growth.

## 7. Measurement and unit economics

Revenue alone is not a sufficient measure of success. Track a balanced set of farmer-outcome, trust, growth, and financial metrics:

**Farmer value and trust**
- Farmer satisfaction and issue-resolution quality.
- Repeat use and retention, segmented by farmer size and region.
- Reported usefulness and successful outcomes where measurable.
- Complaints about pressure, misleading advice, privacy, or unfair treatment.

**Organic growth**
- Voluntary referral rate.
- Share of new active farmers from referrals.
- Referred-user activation and retention.
- Referral fraud and spam reports.

**Commercial health**
- Conversion to optional paid services.
- Gross merchandise value and completed transaction rate.
- Revenue and gross margin by product/service line.
- Refunds, cancellations, disputes, and supplier quality.

**Sustainability**
- AI and infrastructure cost per active farmer.
- Cost of free foundational service by cohort.
- Support and expert-service operating costs.
- Contribution margin and customer acquisition cost.
- Service availability and performance under weak connectivity.

Do not optimize a single metric in isolation. For example, higher conversion is not success if farmer trust or advice quality declines. Report free-service cost transparently internally and use it to plan capacity—not to guilt users.

## 8. Implementation sequence

1. **Define the free-service boundary:** publish what every farmer can use without purchase, plus fair infrastructure limits and support behavior.
2. **Protect advice integrity:** establish commercial disclosure, recommendation independence, and review processes for conflicts of interest.
3. **Make optional revenue reliable:** improve marketplace trust, supplier verification, transaction transparency, and expert-service quality.
4. **Implement Green Credit correctly:** define metering, ledger, reward, expiry, reversal, and abuse controls before enabling real balances or purchase-linked rewards.
5. **Introduce referrals carefully:** pilot voluntary referral tools and evaluate retained-user quality, fraud, and farmer sentiment.
6. **Scale by evidence:** expand regions, partners, and capacity only when service quality, unit economics, and farmer outcomes support it.

## 9. Governance and release gates

Before a feature or commercial partnership launches, the responsible team should answer:

- Does it preserve access to foundational help without purchase?
- Could it bias agricultural advice or create a conflict of interest?
- Are price, commission, sponsorship, and reward terms clear?
- Does it respect farmer privacy and choice?
- Does it work for small farmers and users with limited connectivity?
- Are operating costs, support capacity, and abuse risks understood?
- Can the team measure farmer value as well as revenue?

Features involving real payments, production credit balances, customer-data sharing, or paid third-party services require separate technical, legal, security, and operational review. This strategy document does not authorize production activation, database migrations, paid services, or changes to live customer data.

## 10. The SARINA commitment

**We earn from farmers’ trust, not from their need or hardship.**

A farmer who cannot or does not buy remains part of the community and deserves respectful, useful foundational service. SARINA succeeds when farmers become stronger, better informed, and more economically connected—and choose to support the ecosystem because it has genuinely supported them.

**No farmer is alone.**
